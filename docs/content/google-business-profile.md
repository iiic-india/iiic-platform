## Reviews: how they are asked for

Reviews on Google are third-party proof and a local ranking signal. Google forbids paying or rewarding for them, asking only happy clients, and "review gating" (sending only the satisfied to Google), so IIIC asks evenly and takes what comes.

- **Who is asked:** every consultancy client at the end of an engagement, and every course and workshop participant aged 18 or over at its end, all of them, not only those who seemed pleased.
- **Who is not asked:** anyone under 18, since a review request is treated as promotional and nothing promotional goes to under-18s (`06`; `08` §3). And counselling clients: nothing goes to them through session messages, notes, reminders or the booking automations (`CLAUDE.md` #3), and on Google a reviewer's name and photograph are public, which would show that a person had counselling. A counselling client who chooses to review on their own is not discouraged; IIIC never prompts it.
- **How:** in person, or by a short personal message from the person who did the work, with the profile's "Ask for reviews" link (from the profile's dashboard). The message goes only to someone who holds the matching marketing consent when it is sent (`06`: anything promotional needs it), once, with no reminder chain and nothing offered in return. The wording follows VOICE.md and is reviewed with the other messages (`13`).
- **No mixing:** a Google review is not a review on the platform. Publishing a review on the site still needs the person's consent under D-37 and I-42 (`review-consent-request.md`); it is never copied from Google without it, and the site never shows rating stars or `AggregateRating` markup (`12` §3).
- **Replies:** IIIC replies to reviews in its own voice. A reply never confirms that someone was a client of counselling, never repeats anything from a session, and never offers anything.
- **No fake reviews:** none from staff, friends or family presented as clients, and none bought (`08` §6).

## Updates (posts)

- **Cadence:** two updates a month, which IIIC can keep; a missed month is better than filler.
- **What:** a new Journal article (its question as the update's first line, linking to the article); an upcoming info session or workshop as an event update with its real dates (I-13); a factual note about IIIC's work (a university workshop, a new service once I-11 names it).
- **Never:** an "offer" update or any price or discount (D-21); false urgency ("limited seats", "hurry"); "best" or "first"; anything from or about a counselling session; a client's name without consent.
- **Photos in updates:** the same rules as the photo checklist.
