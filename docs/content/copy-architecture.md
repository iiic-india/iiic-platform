# Copy architecture · every page, section by section

The enhanced copy system for the platform: Falak's copy system (3 November 2026) as the base layer, reconciled with the platform plan, the brand kit, the compliance frame and the sources IIIC has supplied. It says, for every public page, what each section is for, which question of the reader it answers, which copy exists for it and where it came from, and what is still missing.

**How to read the labels.** `[IIIC]` the line was supplied by IIIC and is used verbatim (`sources/`). `[CS]` a candidate line from the copy system, used once IIIC approves it (I-41). `[proposal]` a line proposed by the platform team for IIIC to accept, change or reject; it is never a fact, only wording. `[[TODO: …]]` a fact or text nobody has supplied, rendered as a placeholder with the input ID. Everything follows `src/brand/VOICE.md` 1.0.3, including the writing standard.

## 1. The rhythm every page keeps

Recognise → Understand → Trust → Act (the copy system's four steps, adopted). In the platform's terms:

| Step | What the section does | The kit pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recognise | Opens with the reader's situation in words they would use themselves; the headline is often their question | Hero; "Does this feel familiar?" list |
| Understand | Shows the framework's logic in one or two paragraphs; one idea per paragraph | Prose block; the Object/Subject comparison; `.iiic-steps` |
| Trust | Facts with sources (years, work done, institutions) and, beside each section that makes a claim, the words of someone who lived it: a consented review with name, designation, place, programme and year (§6; D-37); or an honest empty state | Proof cards beside the text (`11` §3.8); counters with "how we count"; empty state |
| Act | One action, repeated once in the closing band with the same wording | Primary button; the closing yellow band with the Sanskrit line |

Rules carried from the copy system: the question test (a section that answers a question nobody asked is cut); one call to action per page; the service-page template (Hero → Does this feel familiar? → Story → Method → What you gain → Who it is for → Outcome band → Call to action); आत्मानं विद्धि once per page, in the closing band, set in ink on yellow.

## 6. Reviews (D-37)

Reviews of Gaurang Mistry's programmes, earlier and new, are part of IIIC's proof: the programmes are the same, now under IIIC. This section says what a review shows, where it goes, what it must pass before anyone is asked for consent, and how the reviews inform the copy.

**What a review shows.** The words as written, or an excerpt the person approved (marked with an ellipsis); then the name in the form the person chose (full name · first name and surname initial · initials), their designation, the organisation only if they agreed, the place, and the programme and year as it was ("Leadership programme, 2016"). A review written in Gujarati or Hindi shows the original, with `lang` set, and an English translation the person approved, labelled "Translated from Gujarati" (or Hindi). A photograph or a LinkedIn link appears only if the person agreed to each. If the writer is a friend or relative of Gaurang Mistry, or works or worked with IIIC, the card says so after the caption ("Friend of Gaurang Mistry"): India's endorsement guidance asks for a material connection to be disclosed (`08` §6).

**How a voice is chosen.** Each slot has a theme: the claim its section makes. The editor pins a review whose words speak to that claim; a review appears once per page; the selection changes only when the editor changes it, never on a timer.

**Asking for reviews.** New reviews are asked for with the request message in `review-consent-request.md`, which asks for the programme, the year and what changed. The first replies followed four headings (name, designation, city, and feedback and experience), and none of the eight received so far names a programme or a year (`outcomes-map.md`).

**Screening, before a consent request is sent.** A review goes to its writer for consent only if every test passes:

1. It is genuine: IIIC holds the original (a letter, a message, a form, a post, a recording).
2. It names a real programme and its year.
3. It describes the person's own experience.
4. It states no diagnosis, cure or clinical result, and claims nothing IIIC could not claim itself, such as a prediction (`08` §6–7).
5. It names no other private person or their circumstances.
6. The person is an adult now and consents themselves; for anyone under 18, a parent consents and the card shows initials only.
7. It is not about the AnkVishwa assessment or predictions (D-26).
8. An excerpt, if one is proposed, keeps the meaning of the whole.

A review that fails a test stays on file, unpublished, with the reason recorded.

**Consent.** The person sees exactly what will appear and chooses how their name is shown, or declines (`review-consent-request.md`); their reply is the evidence, stored with the review. Asking them to confirm they still stand by what they wrote is what keeps an older review "reasonably current" (`08` §6), and the year stays on the card. A withdrawal unpublishes the review at once (V2).

**What the reviews teach.** Every review is read in full and summarised in `outcomes-map.md` (latest reading: 19 November 2026, eight reviews): the outcomes people describe in their own words, grouped into themes, how often each appears, in which programme, audience and decade, with the review references as evidence. The map is used to choose which voice sits beside which section, to set the theme tags behind Proof's filters, and to propose outcome lines and "Does this feel familiar?" lines drawn from what clients actually said (`[proposal]`, approved under I-41). It does not change what IIIC is, its three applications or the framework's terms (§2–3): those stay as IIIC has defined them. No figure from the map is published unless it says what was counted and since when (VOICE.md, the proof rule).
