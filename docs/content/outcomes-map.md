## What the readings change

1. **Ask for the programme, the year and what changed.** The 14 November replies follow the same four headings: name, designation, city, and "Feedback & Experience". None of the eight names a programme or a year, and only R-001 describes a change in what the writer does. New requests use the message in `review-consent-request.md`, sent to people who took a programme.
2. **Readings are what people remember first.** Six of the eight describe a date-of-birth reading or a prediction. They stay on file and off the site: D-26 keeps the AnkVishwa side to the founder's biography, and test 4 would stop them anyway, since a review cannot claim what IIIC could not claim itself, and IIIC does not claim to predict.
3. **The outcomes people value are the ones IIIC names, but the evidence is from readings.** R-007 and R-008 describe understanding themselves and deciding better; IIIC's philosophy document names decisiveness and "to help individuals understand themselves" as what its work is for. Reviews of the programmes that describe the same change are the evidence the site needs; until they exist, no outcome line rests on these two.
4. **People come back.** Three of the eight describe guidance over time rather than a single session (R-004, R-007, R-008), and R-007 came through a friend's referral.
5. **Connections are common.** Two writers know him personally: R-004 calls him "Gaurang Uncle", and R-005, writing in Gujarati, calls him a friend. A published card discloses the connection (§6; `08` §6), so the intake sheet records one for every review.
6. **Check what people were asked.** R-008 opens "Yes,", as if answering a question. Requests ask open questions only (`review-consent-request.md`).
7. **Gujarati reviews need checked translations.** Two of the eight are in Gujarati. The card shows the original with a translation the writer approved; the working translations in the intake sheet are checked by a Gujarati speaker on the team first.
8. **Designations need a word with the writer.** One wrote "not working", one gave a degree. The consent message already asks what to show.
9. **Still too thin for outcome lines.** One review that can inform the site describes a change and one a gain in understanding. No outcome line or "Does this feel familiar?" line is proposed yet. Proposals start when at least three reviews that pass screening describe the same outcome.
