# 16 · Booking and payments: the design (M3)

The design M3 is built to: the data, the states, the money rules, the double-booking guard, the module boundaries and who builds what. Written 22 November 2026 on branch `m3/foundation`, which holds the schema, its two migrations, the contracts in `src/lib/booking`, `src/lib/payments` and `src/lib/calendar`, the fakes and the first tests. The target is M3's "Done when" (`10`): end-to-end tests in test mode for a paid booking, a free booking, a payment failure, hold expiry, a reschedule, a cancellation with refund, and two people paying for one slot (exactly one confirmed, the other refunded); an invoice PDF that matches the print template. Sources: `03` F3–F4, `04`, `05` (Calendar, Bookings, Money), `06` (B1–B14), `07`, `08` §3–§5, §7, §9, D-02, D-03, D-09, D-13, D-16, D-21–D-24. Where this document and those differ, they win, and this one is corrected.

## 1. Rules this design keeps

- A payment is confirmed only by a verified webhook or a server-side check of the order with the gateway, each processed once by its id; the browser's return from checkout confirms nothing (CLAUDE.md #5).
- Money is integer paise everywhere: collections, functions, the gateway. Totals and tax are computed on the server from the price in force; no price, rate, threshold, duration, policy or SAC code is written in code (CLAUDE.md #1). Those are IIIC's and live in the admin; seed and test values say "fake".
- No price appears on a page, an email, an invite or a template while `prices_public` is off (D-21); the one exception is the amount on a payment link staff send, which the client sees before paying (D-24).
- Counselling is private: a booking holds when and what, never what is said; domain events carry ids only; nothing about a session reaches analytics, logs, a practitioner's calendar beyond a first name, or a message offer (CLAUDE.md #3).
- Consent is recorded before the data it covers, in the same transaction; every consent is its own unticked box (CLAUDE.md #4, #9). Times are stored in UTC and shown in India time; phones in E.164 (#8). Every route and custom view checks the user and role first (#6).

## 3. The states

Lists and allowed moves live in `src/lib/booking/states.ts` and `src/lib/payments/states.ts`; the collections and the constraint are built from them, and tests hold them together. Who may make a move is the builders'.

**Booking.** Created as `requested` (a time request), `held` (a hold) or `confirmed` (no payment at booking, or the office books it). `held` → `pendingPayment` (checkout opened) → `confirmed` (capture). `held` or `pendingPayment` → `expired` (the hold ran out) or `cancelledByClient` (they left). `expired` → `confirmed` only when a late payment arrives and the slot is still free; otherwise the payment is refunded. `requested` → `confirmed` (the office sets the agreed time, which the constraint checks) or cancelled by either side. `confirmed` → `completed` · `noShow` · `cancelledByClient` · `cancelledByIiic` · `rescheduled` (a reschedule creates a new booking pointing back with `rescheduledFrom`). The last five are final. `expired` is new: docs/04's list has no word for a lapsed hold (proposed, §10).

**Hold.** Not a table of its own: a booking in `held` or `pendingPayment` with `holdExpiresAt` = now + hold minutes (the service's, else Settings'; default 10, docs/03). It ends converted (`confirmed`), expired or abandoned. Expiry is applied lazily, in the transaction that places a new hold (any overlapping hold past its time is moved to `expired` first), and by the B4 sweep; a timer is shown only for this real hold and never resets (`08` §5).

**Time request.** A booking in `requested` with no time; it blocks nothing. The office agrees a time with the person and confirms it (B2 fires, the constraint checks the time). R1 has no "offered, waiting for the client's yes" step (proposed, §10).

**Booking payment status.** `notRequired` (free) · `unpaid` · `linkSent` · `paid` · `partRefunded` · `refunded`, moved by the payments module only.

**Payment.** `created` → `authorized` → `captured`; `created` → `captured` (automatic capture); `created` or `authorized` → `failed`, and a failed order may still be paid by a second attempt; `created` → `expired` · `cancelled` (links). `captured` → `partRefunded` → `refunded`. A captured payment never goes back.

**Refund.** `requested` → `approved` (within the threshold, or the platform's own B10 refund) or `awaitingApproval` → `approved` | `declined` (an owner). `approved` → `processing` (sent to the gateway) → `processed` | `failed`; a failed one is sent again by hand.

**Invoice.** `pending` → `issued` | `failed` (retried by its job) ; `issued` → `void` when cancelled in Zoho Books.

## 4. The double-booking guard

docs/04 asks for an exclusion constraint on practitioner and time range for held, pending-payment, confirmed and requested bookings. It is created by `src/migrations/20261122_150505_bookings_no_overlap.ts`:

```sql
CREATE EXTENSION IF NOT EXISTS btree_gist;
ALTER TABLE "bookings" ADD CONSTRAINT "bookings_ends_after_start"
  CHECK ("starts_at" IS NULL OR "ends_at" IS NULL OR "ends_at" > "starts_at");
ALTER TABLE "bookings" ADD CONSTRAINT "bookings_no_overlap"
  EXCLUDE USING gist ("practitioner_id" WITH =, tstzrange("starts_at", "ends_at", '[)') WITH &&)
  WHERE ("practitioner_id" IS NOT NULL AND "starts_at" IS NOT NULL AND "ends_at" IS NOT NULL
         AND "status" IN ('requested', 'held', 'pendingPayment', 'confirmed'));
```

**How it is made and kept.** Payload generates tables and indexes, not exclusion constraints, and generated migrations are never edited. So the scaffold is one generated migration (`m3_foundation`), and the constraint is a second, blank one made with `pnpm migrate:create bookings_no_overlap --force-accept-warning` and written by hand, as the audit log's trigger was (D-43). It is never edited; a change is a new migration. Drizzle's snapshots do not know the constraint, so later generated migrations leave it alone; one that drops or retypes a column it uses fails loudly in Postgres rather than losing it. Guards: a unit test holds the migration's state list to `SLOT_BLOCKING_STATUSES`; an integration test checks the constraint exists and refuses an overlap (`tests/int/bookingsConstraint.int.spec.ts`). This mechanism is proposed in §10 for IIIC to confirm.

**What it means.** Ranges are half-open, so 10:00–10:30 and 10:30–11:00 coexist; the buffer after a session is the slot engine's rule, not the database's. A time request without a time, and a fixed-time session's booking (no practitioner), are outside it; an info session's capacity is checked with a row lock on its event. A refused insert or update raises SQLSTATE `23P01`, which the booking module turns into `{ code: 'slot-taken' }`. Postgres aborts a transaction on a refused statement and Payload's own create and update roll the request's transaction back when they fail, so every booking write the guard may refuse runs in a savepoint (`src/lib/booking/guard.ts`): a refusal undoes that write only and the caller's transaction stays usable, so the payments module records the event and requests the B10 refund in the same transaction. An operation that started its own transaction rolls it back when it answers with an error. Holds, confirmations and changes also take the booking's row lock (`FOR UPDATE`), so the B4 sweep and a late payment never both act on one booking, and placing a time takes the practitioner's advisory lock, so two people booking one practitioner are served in turn and each sees the other's buffer; the constraint still has the last word. Busy blocks from Google are not in the constraint: the engine subtracts them, and a clash with a later calendar entry is the practitioner's to move.

**The whole transaction goes with a refusal.** Payload rolls back and forgets the request's transaction when any operation inside it throws, even one that did not open it (`killTransaction` in every operation's error path), and a savepoint cannot stop it. So a caller cannot carry on in the same transaction after the constraint refuses a write: what it wrote before is gone, and what it writes after would commit piece by piece. The payments module therefore asks first, in its own transaction, whether a lapsed hold's time is taken (`slotTakenByAnother` in `src/lib/payments/store.ts`) and confirms only when it is free; a true race still reaches the constraint, the event's transaction ends, and the event is retried and finds the time taken. After ConfirmBooking returns, it checks that its transaction is still open and throws if not. The booking module's own operations meet the same rule (builder A).

## 5. Money and payments

**Prices.** A service's options hold the all-in price in paise and the GST flag (M2); the service holds its SAC code and its GST rate in basis points (I-29). Every save of a service brings `price-history` in line (`src/lib/payments/prices.ts`, the services collection's `afterChange`): an option whose price, GST flag, rate, label or length changed has its open row closed and a new one opened; a removed or unpriced option's row is closed; an unchanged save changes nothing. At step 3 the server copies the price in force into the booking (`price.totalPaise`, `taxPaise`, rate, SAC) from `quotePrice` (`no-price` while none is set, `rate-missing` while a priced option has no GST rate, so no tax is guessed); that total is what step 1 showed and never grows (`08` §5). A session whose price is not set books without payment, as while prices are hidden. A price the CA has set as before tax (the GST box unticked) has its tax added on top, rounded half up (`splitExclusive`).

**Tax.** An all-in total splits as taxable = total × 10000 / (10000 + rate), rounded half up to the paise, tax = total − taxable (contract `SplitInclusive`); CGST and SGST halve the tax within Gujarat (the odd paise to CGST) and IGST applies between states, the supplier's state read from the GSTIN in Settings (contract `SplitGstHeads`). Both rounding rules are the CA's to confirm (§10).

**What an invoice and a receipt record.** IIIC is GST-registered (D-13), so Zoho Books issues a tax invoice (or a receipt voucher), and a credit note or refund voucher on a refund; which one, and when, is the CA's (I-29). Each is mirrored in `invoices` with the buyer as named at issue, each line with SAC, rate, taxable value and tax heads, the totals in paise as the platform computed them (which must equal Zoho's; a mismatch fails the job), Zoho's id and number, and the PDF's key in the private bucket. Invoices are anonymised, never deleted, when a person is erased (`04`).

**Checkout (pay at booking).** Entering step 4 places the hold; opening checkout creates one Razorpay order per booking (receipt `booking:<id>:checkout`, the payment's unique key; a second click finds it), with the booking row locked, and moves the booking to `pendingPayment`; a gateway that does not answer leaves the hold as it was. Automatic capture is Razorpay's account setting (Dashboard → payment capture), not a field of the order, and must be on. The browser gets the order id and the public key id only (`checkoutParams`). On return, the confirmation page asks the server to check the order with the gateway once (`verifyReturnedPayment`): only when the checkout signature is right, and any capture found is processed under the poll's event id; otherwise it waits for the webhook.

**The webhook** (`/api/webhooks/razorpay`, builder B): read the raw body (size-limited), verify `X-Razorpay-Signature` with the SDK's `validateWebhookSignature` and the webhook secret, read `X-Razorpay-Event-Id`, parse only the needed fields with Zod (`VerifiedGatewayEvent`). Then, in one transaction: claim the event with `INSERT … ON CONFLICT ("gateway", "event_id") DO NOTHING RETURNING id`; no row back means it is there already, a **replay**: answer 200 and do nothing. A concurrent duplicate waits on the unique index until the first commits, then gets no row and is answered 200 as a replay, without the refused write that would end its transaction (§4); if the first rolled back, the second claims it. Otherwise act on the event and write its outcome. If acting fails, the transaction rolls back (the event row with it) and the route answers 500, so Razorpay retries (for 24 hours). Acting is itself idempotent: the payment row is locked first, a capture on a payment already captured is `ignored`, a late or repeated event is `ignored` (`out-of-order`, `already-…`), and an amount or currency that differs from the payment's is `flagged` and confirms nothing. What each event does is decided by a pure plan (`src/lib/payments/eventPlan.ts`) and applied by `webhook.ts`; the route's answers are in `webhookHandler.ts` (401 bad signature, 400 unreadable body or missing event id, 200 for an event the platform does not take, 503 while payments are off, 413 above 64 KB). Events taken: `payment.authorized`, `payment.captured`, `payment.failed`, `order.paid`, `refund.processed`, `refund.failed`, `payment_link.paid`, `payment_link.expired`, `payment_link.cancelled`. Logs hold the event type and outcome, never the body.

**Capture → confirmation, and the race.** A capture marks the payment captured and the booking `paid`, emits `payment.captured`, and calls `ConfirmBooking` (cause `paymentCaptured`). If the hold had expired and someone else holds the time now (asked first, §4), the payment module requests a full refund in the platform's name (reason `slotTaken`, key `slotTaken:<paymentId>`, approved at once, B10), flags the event `slot-taken` and emits `booking.slotLost` so M6 offers the nearest free times; the refund is sent after the event's transaction commits. Two captures racing for one slot: exactly one is confirmed, the other refunded; if both reach the constraint at once, ConfirmBooking's write runs in a savepoint (`guarded`, `src/lib/booking/guard.ts`), so the loser gets `slot-taken` with its transaction still open and is refunded in it (should a write ever end the transaction, the event is thrown back and its retry finds the time taken). `src/lib/payments/wiring.ts` joins the module to the booking module's `confirmBooking` and `emit`; `tests/int/payments.int.spec.ts` proves both races. A capture for a booking cancelled, completed or moved meanwhile is kept and flagged (`booking-<status>`) for the office, with no automatic refund (proposed).

**Polling and reconciliation** (M3 owns both). `B2-poll-pending-orders` asks the gateway about checkout orders still `created`, `authorized` or `failed` between five minutes and two days old and processes any capture as an event of source `poll` (event id `poll:<gateway payment id>:captured`, so it is acted on once, whoever reports it first), each in a transaction of its own; it also sends refunds left `approved` for two minutes. `B11-reconcile` compares a window's captured payments with the platform's (a day in India given as `day`, else the last 26 hours) and stores each mismatch once as a flagged `reconciliation` event (`reconcile:<gateway payment id>:<finding>`: `unknown-capture`, `missed-capture`, `amount-mismatch`, `booking-not-paid`, `refund-outside-platform`), emitting `reconciliation.mismatch`; it acts on nothing. A partial refund made in the dashboard is not seen, because the gateway's payment list carries no refunded amount (proposed: add it to `GatewayPayment`). Both are tasks, loaded when they run so the Payload CLI never loads the gateway's `server-only` module; M6's cron runner schedules them (daily 23:00 for B11); until then an owner runs them through the jobs API. Their slugs join the jobs table's enum, which needs a migration at merge.

**Payment links (D-24, B13).** Staff (owner or operator) send one from a confirmed booking with a price; never for a free service or a booking already paid. The gateway's own SMS and email are off (`notify: false`), no contact detail goes to the gateway, and the link's description is the booking reference only; the platform's message carries the link (M6, on `payment.linkSent`). One open link per booking: sending again cancels the old one at the gateway first (receipts `booking:<id>:link:<n>`, Razorpay's reference id, unique there too). A link counts for the booking's whole chain of reschedules, where the payment stays: none is sent when a payment of the chain is captured, sending one cancels any open link of the chain, and a cancellation or a reschedule (the office's or the client's) closes the chain's open links in its own transaction, the bookings left `linkSent` going back to `unpaid`; a link the gateway cannot close (down, or paid meanwhile) refuses the change, which is tried again (`closeOpenLinks`, `links.ts`). `payment_link.paid` marks the payment captured and the booking `paid`; an expired or cancelled link moves the booking from `linkSent` back to `unpaid`.

**Refunds.** The office requests one (from a cancellation, with the amount the service's rule gives shown first, or by hand); it is approved at once when the amount is at or below `refundApprovalThresholdPaise`, else it waits for an owner (who decides with their two-factor code entered); with no threshold set, every refund waits, whoever asked. A request never promises more than the payment can give back: its amount, less what went back and what open refunds hold (`over-refund`); one key is one refund. The same rules hold for a refund made in the admin or the REST API (the refunds collection's hooks): the platform sets its key, who asked and its state, and through the API a refund can only be approved or declined by an owner. Approval does not send it from inside the approving transaction: `sendRefund` runs after it commits (the webhook route for B10, the admin's action in wave 2, and the poll for anything left `approved`), moves the refund to `processing` and commits before calling the gateway, so nothing is sent twice; it goes always to the original method, with the refund's key as its receipt, and the gateway's event finds a refund sent but not yet recorded by that receipt. `refund.processed` (or the gateway's own answer) moves it to `processed`, adds to the payment's `refundedPaise` (never past the amount: `over-refund`), moves the payment and the booking to `partRefunded` or `refunded`, and emits `refund.processed` (the client's message and the credit note follow). A cancellation by IIIC refunds in full (D-16's symmetry).

## 6. The slot engine and the booking module

Pure functions, no database, in `src/lib/booking/` (types in `types.ts`):

- `GenerateSlots(query: SlotQuery) → SlotDay[]` (`slots.ts`). Input: `now`, `durationMinutes`, rules (notice hours, horizon days, buffer minutes), the practitioner's `WeeklyHours[]` (weekday, spans, validFrom, validTo), `DayException[]`, and `taken: Interval[]` (busy blocks and slot-blocking bookings, lapsed holds left out). Starts run from each span's start every `duration + buffer`; a start is offered when [start, start + duration + buffer) fits the span, starts at or after now + notice, falls on or before the horizon's last day (today in India plus the horizon's days), and meets nothing taken, where each taken time also keeps the buffer after it, so two sessions are a buffer apart whatever their lengths and whichever came first. An exception replaces the day's hours (closed: none); when two sets of hours are valid on one day, the one that started last holds. Output: `{ day: 'YYYY-MM-DD', starts: Date[] }` per day in India, earliest first, empty days left out. Wall-clock times become instants with `@date-fns/tz` (`TZDate` in Asia/Kolkata); no hand-made offsets.
- `ResolveRules(service, defaults) → BookingRules | rule-missing`: the service's rule, else Settings'; a rule neither sets stops the flow from offering times.
- `CanClientReschedule({ now, startsAt, rescheduleWindowHours, status })` and `RefundForCancellation({ now, startsAt, paidPaise, steps, by })`: with no window or no steps set (D-16 open), the client is sent to IIIC instead (F4) and nothing is refunded automatically.
- Contracts that touch the database (`operations.ts`): `PlaceHold`, `ConfirmBooking` (the one way into `confirmed`), `ExpireHolds`, `RequestTime`, `RescheduleBooking`, `CancelBooking`, each returning a typed `Result` (`src/lib/result.ts`).

How builder A built them (`src/lib/booking/`, server-only except the pure files): `holds.ts` (PlaceHold, ExpireHolds, `startPayment` for checkout, `extendHold`, which keeps a hold once more when the gateway was down, docs/03 F3), `confirm.ts` (ConfirmBooking; `bookWithoutPayment`, which creates a booking confirmed at once, D-24), `timeRequests.ts`, `changes.ts`, `guard.ts`, `load.ts`, `write.ts`, `emit.ts`. The booking module never computes money: the price copied into a booking is the payments module's quote, passed in by the server (`PriceSource`). A client may cancel a booking they have not paid for, before it starts; a paid one only under a refund rule (proposed, §10). The server functions the flow and the office call are in `api.ts`, each validating its input with Zod and checking who asks first: anyone lists times (none until the practitioner's calendar is connected), books (`bookSlot`: a hold when payment is taken at booking and the session is priced, else confirmed at once) and asks for a time, for an offered service, with the person and the price source passed by the flow's own server code, never the browser; the manage link's token opens, moves and cancels its booking; the office (owner or operator with the two-factor code) confirms time requests and moves or cancels any booking. Answers carry no price, person, note or hash. In the admin, the collection gives a booking its reference when the office books one, its end from its length, and lets a staff edit move the state only along the table (`bookingHooks.ts`). B4 is `src/jobs/booking.ts` (`B4-expire-holds`).

Left to builder A, named here: the reference (`IIIC-` + S, C or I + `-` + five random digits from `crypto.randomInt`, unique index, retried on collision; an identifier, never enough to read a booking); the manage token (32 random bytes, base64url, sent once; its SHA-256 kept in `manageTokenHash`; valid until the booking ends; a reschedule issues a new one); the confirmation page shows details only to the browser that booked (a short-lived httpOnly cookie bound to the checkout key), anyone else sees that a booking exists and that the details are in their email.

## 7. Calendars

`CalendarProvider` (`src/lib/calendar/provider.ts`): `freeBusy`, `createEvent`, `updateEvent`, `deleteEvent`, `watch`, `stopWatch`, each returning a `Result`. Google's implementation (`google.ts`) uses `@googleapis/calendar` with a Workspace service account and domain-wide delegation limited to two scopes, `calendar.events` and `calendar.freebusy` (no per-practitioner tokens are stored; proposed, §10). The account acts as the calendar's own address, and only an address on `GOOGLE_WORKSPACE_DOMAIN`; any other calendar is refused as `forbidden`. Without the three Google settings, and on every Vercel deployment but production (previews share production's database), there is no calendar: each step answers `not-configured` and bookings go on without it (`config.ts`, `calendarFor.ts`). Busy times come from the **free/busy** endpoint, which returns start and end only, so no event's title or guests are ever read (D-22, B14). `RefreshBusyBlocks` (`sync.ts`) reads [now, the end of the longest horizon's last day in India] (the settings' and every active service's horizon; none set, nothing is read), clips and merges the answer (`busy.ts`) and makes the practitioner's blocks match in one transaction, removing those no longer busy and adding the new; it runs on each change notification and every 15 minutes. Change notifications (`/api/webhooks/google-calendar`, `notifications.ts`) are watched only while `GOOGLE_CALENDAR_WEBHOOK_TOKEN` is set and the site is on https: each channel gets its own random 256-bit token, the connection keeps its SHA-256 in `channelTokenHash`, and a notification is believed only when its channel, token and resource all match (an unknown channel is answered 404, a wrong token 401; a calendar that fails to answer is caught up by the sweep, not by Google's retries). Channels are renewed a day before they end; a paused connection's channel is stopped by the sweep. `SyncBookingEvent` (`sync.ts`) writes a confirmed booking as a private event without guests or description: time, service, the client's first name, the reference (`bookingEvent.ts`), and a Meet link (online) or the office address. The event's Google id is made from `<reference>@<host>`, so a retried insert finds the event it made. A reschedule moves the replaced booking's event (same practitioner) or removes it and writes a new one (another practitioner); a cancellation or a lapsed hold removes it; the event id and Meet link are kept on the booking. It runs as the retried job `B2-B7-sync-calendar-event` after the booking commits, so a calendar failure never undoes a booking; the booking module's `emit` queues it on `booking.confirmed`, `booking.rescheduled` and `booking.cancelled` (`CALENDAR_SUBSCRIPTIONS` in `queue.ts`). A Meet link made earlier stays on the practitioner's entry when a booking changes to the office (the client's invite and the booking drop it). The client's invite is the platform's `.ics` built with `ics` (`ics.ts`, `bookingInvite.ts`): UID `<first reference>@<host>`, where the first reference is that of the first booking in the chain of reschedules, so the UID never changes; SEQUENCE is the number of reschedules since, and a cancellation sends METHOD:CANCEL with the next number; ORGANIZER (the sending address) and the client as the only ATTENDEE, not asked to reply, when the sender names them (RFC 5546 asks both of a REQUEST and a CANCEL). Each practitioner also gets a private feed of their own bookings (`feed.ts`, `/api/calendar/feed/[token]`): confirmed, completed and no-show bookings from the start of today in India, with the same words as their Google entry, `CLASS:PRIVATE`; the token is 128 random bits, its hash on the connection; an owner with their code makes a new address (`POST /api/calendar-connections/:id/feed-link`, which replaces the old) or revokes it (`DELETE`); pointing the connection at another practitioner or another calendar revokes it too (`connectionHooks.ts`), so an old address never shows someone else's bookings; a revoked, replaced or unknown token, or a practitioner whose staff account is switched off, gets the same 404. `FakeCalendar` keeps busy times and written events in memory, and counts written events as busy, as Google does. Until a practitioner has hours and a connected calendar (I-24), no free time is offered for them; bookings still show in the admin.

## 9. Events for M6

`emit(req, event)` (`src/lib/booking/events.ts`) queues a Payload job per subscribed task in the same transaction, with the key `<event>:<id>`. M3 subscribes three tasks (`LISTENERS` in `emit.ts`): `booking.confirmed`, `booking.rescheduled` and `booking.cancelled` queue `B2-B7-sync-calendar-event` (from `CALENDAR_SUBSCRIPTIONS`), `payment.captured` queues `B3-issue-invoice` and `refund.processed` queues `B7-issue-credit-note` (from `DOCUMENT_SUBSCRIPTIONS`, `src/lib/invoicing/queue.ts`); every other subscription is M6's. `calendar.changed` is emitted by `RefreshBusyBlocks` in the transaction that changes a practitioner's busy blocks, only when they changed. Events carry ids only: `booking.held` (B1), `booking.holdExpired` (B4), `booking.requested` (B12), `booking.confirmed` with its cause (B2, B3a, B5, B6, P2), `booking.rescheduled` (B7), `booking.cancelled` (B7), `booking.completed` (B8), `booking.noShow` (B9), `booking.slotLost` (B10), `payment.captured` (B3), `payment.failed`, `payment.linkSent` (B13), `refund.awaitingApproval` (Today), `refund.processed` (B7, the credit note), `reconciliation.mismatch` (B11), `calendar.changed` (B14). M3 itself runs the calendar write, the poll, B11 and the documents; every message is M6's.

## 11. Fakes, environment and the test plan
