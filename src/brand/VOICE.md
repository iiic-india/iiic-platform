# IIIC voice and writing guide · 1.0.3

How IIIC writes on the platform, in email and WhatsApp, in documents and on social. It applies to anyone writing for IIIC, and to Claude Code when it writes interface copy. When a fact is not known, the copy shows a placeholder (`[[TODO: …]]`); it never invents one. The words IIIC has already written, and where each line goes, are in the platform's `docs/content/`.

## One voice, three temperatures

IIIC sounds like a thoughtful teacher who has spent decades on one question: how people in India understand themselves in relation to family, society and work. Calm, exact, warm, never selling hard.

| | Consultancy | Education | Counselling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The reader | A founder or HR lead with a real problem and little time | A learner deciding whether the framework is worth studying | A person or family looking for help, often after trying something that did not work |
| Sounds like | A precise adviser in a boardroom | A serious teacher | A calm, unhurried listener |
| Sentences | Short. Numbers when they are real. | Explanatory, with one idea per paragraph | Plain, gentle, second person |
| Proof | Case studies, measured outcomes, institutional work | Syllabus, certification standard, university work | Credentials, a clear first-session promise, consented stories |
| Example direction | "Your team's numbers have a cause. We map it." | "Learn the Indian theory of the self, and certify in it." | A line that says these sessions are unlike what the person has tried before, without the word "therapy" (wording to be chosen and legally cleared; D-19) |

The example lines are directions from the platform plan, not approved headlines. The plan's counselling line, "Not therapy as you've known it", is on hold: Indian rules reserve clinical titles, and describing IIIC's sessions as a kind of therapy invites that reading.

## Claims: the proof rule

- Every claim can be checked. A number on the site says what was counted and since when, or it is not published.
- Testimonials come only from real clients through the verified flow, with consent recorded; feedback collected before the platform existed (the 2023 workshop cards in IIIC's brochure, a client's written piece) is published only once consent for this use is recorded and the programme and date are shown. Never write one, paraphrase one, or "improve" one beyond spelling. A narrative IIIC writes to illustrate a reader (the founders' page story) is labelled as IIIC's illustration and never presented as a client's story.
- No superlatives or firsts without evidence: not "best", "leading", "world-class", "first", "first ever", "only", "most popular", "most booked", "guaranteed", "proven". The current site calls Subjective Self the "first ever" Indian theory of psychology; that claim needs evidence (and a lawyer's view) before the platform repeats it.
- Counselling is described as non-clinical. Do not promise outcomes ("you will feel better", "cure", "heal"), do not diagnose, and do not use clinical titles without the licence behind them.
- Do not imply university affiliation, accreditation or degree equivalence. The PDEU workshop is described exactly as it happened: "Subjective Self", a two-day workshop on self-knowledge at Pandit Deendayal Energy University, 3–4 April 2023 (as listed on iiicedu.com).
- The Map is a reflection tool, not a psychological test. Write "reflect", "map", "see", not "diagnose", "measure your personality" or "assess your mental health".

## Writing standard

Nothing on the platform may read like generated filler (Yash/Aum, 4 November 2026). The standard, which the reviewer applies to every user-facing string:

1. **Start where the reader is.** The first line names their situation in words they would use (the question they carry, the desk, the phone, the quiet house) before IIIC says anything about itself. The founders' page in `docs/content/` is the model.
2. **One idea per paragraph.** Short paragraphs; sentences of different lengths; a long sentence earns its place by carrying one thought.
3. **Concrete beats abstract.** A loan co-signed, a cabin, a phone that rings at nine; not "challenges", "solutions", "growth". Abstract nouns appear when they are the framework's own terms: discipline, accountability, decisiveness, clarity, purpose.
4. **No triplets of abstractions.** "Clarity, purpose and growth" is filler. "Discipline, accountability and decisiveness" is the framework's own list and appears at most once per page, in the outcome band.
5. **Say what is true, with its source.** Every number is counted; every claim can be checked (the proof rule). Where there is no fact, write nothing rather than something vague.
6. **The question test.** Each section answers a question the reader already has. If no reader would ask it, cut it.
7. **Recognition, not manipulation.** The headline may be the reader's own question. Fear, scarcity, flattery and guilt are out (the dark-pattern rules apply to copy too).
8. **The framework's words, not borrowed ones.** Subject and object; the self in connection with family, society, thought and emotion; observe, understand, choose, own. Not the vocabulary of self-help or corporate training.
9. **Remove the tells:** chains of long dashes; "In today's fast-paced world"; "Imagine…"; "Welcome to…"; "It's not just X, it's Y"; delve, navigate, landscape, tapestry, unlock, elevate, empower, seamless, holistic; "journey" outside IIIC's own lines; rows of rhetorical questions; three-word fragment headlines ("Clarity. Purpose. Growth."); lists of generic benefits; exclamation marks; emojis; a closing paragraph that restates the opening; bold on random phrases.
10. **Read it aloud.** If it sounds like a brochure or an announcement, rewrite it as one person speaking to another.
11. **Respect.** The reader is an adult who has already tried things. Never imply they are broken, lost or stuck unless they said so; the "Does this feel familiar?" lines describe situations, not deficiencies.
12. **Sanskrit with meaning.** The signature line, once per page, with its translation; nothing else in Sanskrit or Hindi unless IIIC supplies it with its meaning.
13. **Indian English, Indian context.** ₹, lakh and crore; British spelling; Indian names only when they are real people who agreed.
14. **The last test.** Would the founder say this sentence to a client across a table? Would a sceptical founder believe it? Can every fact be pointed to? If any answer is no, it does not go live.

## Interface copy

| Situation | Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary actions | Verb + object, sentence case, no full stop | Take the Map · Book a session · Book a proposal call · Join a free info session · Enrol · Request a Team Map |
| Secondary actions | Plain verb | No, thanks · Back · Change · Cancel booking |
| Field labels | Noun, sentence case | Full name · Mobile number · Email |
| Helper text | What happens with the answer | "We send your confirmation here." |
| Errors | What happened and how to fix it, no blame | "Enter a 10-digit mobile number." · "That slot was just taken. Pick another time." |
| Empty states | What will appear and why it is empty | "Verified client stories will appear here. We only publish proof tied to a real booking." |
| Confirmations | What is done, what happens next | "Booked. You'll get a confirmation by email, and a reminder before your session." |
| Cross-path cards | One sentence, an opt-in, and a plain "No, thanks" with the same size and weight as the accept button | "Understand the framework behind your sessions: a free info session." |

No exclamation marks in the interface. No dark patterns (India specifies 13; see `docs/08-security-privacy-compliance.md` §5): no fake timers or "only X left" unless true, no pre-ticked consent, no consent bundled with a booking, no fees revealed late, no guilt-trip decline copy, no light-grey "no" buttons, no repeat prompts after "No, thanks", no double negatives in consent wording.

## Email and WhatsApp

- Subject lines and first lines say what the message is: "Your session on Friday, 6 November at 3 PM".
- One message, one purpose, one action.
- WhatsApp messages are short, start with the person's first name, and never carry anything said in a session.
- Reminders state the date, the time, the place or link, and how to reschedule.
- Marketing messages go only to people who opted in, and every one carries a way to stop.

## Counselling copy

- Describe sessions as non-clinical conversations based on the Subjective Self framework; describe people by their real background, exactly as supplied.
- Every counselling page, the booking flow, Map results and counselling emails carry the scope statement (non-clinical; not diagnosis or treatment; not an emergency service) and the crisis numbers: Tele-MANAS 14416 or 1-800-891-4416 (free, 24×7), emergencies 112, children 1098. Re-check the numbers before each release.
- Confidentiality is promised with its legal limits stated (including mandatory reporting where a child may be at risk).

## Certificates and affiliation

- Certificates carry fixed wording approved by a lawyer (draft: "Certificate of Completion — [course]. Issued by Indian Institute of Image Catalysts, a privately owned sole proprietorship, Ahmedabad. Not a degree or diploma; not affiliated with, approved or recognised by UGC, AICTE, any university or any Government body.").
- The footer carries a short statement that IIIC is privately owned and not affiliated with any Government body (wording to be cleared).
- A university workshop, an MoU or an `.edu.in` domain is never presented as recognition.
